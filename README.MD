## My Position on AI

I believe AI is useful when it serves people rather than corporate greed.

I have no problem with AI-generated content when it respects the principles of openness, transparency, and fair use. For example:

- **FOSS projects** that everyone can inspect, modify, and use freely.
- **AI-assisted commits and pull requests** containing a reasonable, reviewable amount of code. I am not going to review 10,000 lines of AI-generated code dumped into a single PR. Break it down, explain what it does, and make it possible to review.

While corporations are using AI to extract more money from people, some people reject the technology entirely on principle. I understand the frustration, but **luddism is a fundamentally weak weapon against corporate power**. Refusing to use a technology does very little to undermine the corporations controlling it.

A much more effective form of resistance is to **use the same technology as a weapon against corporate dependency**.

Look at what developers are already doing: building alternatives to Adobe Creative Cloud so people no longer have to live under perpetual corporate subscriptions; reviving abandoned software and games so they run on modern operating systems or even in VR; creating open-source replacements for expensive proprietary tools; rebuilding libraries that corporations abandoned years ago; and making software that people can actually own, inspect, modify, and redistribute.

**That is how you win.**

At least, that is how you start winning against corporations selling software that is increasingly itself generated by AI.

If you want to make Linux better, don't just complain that Linux is missing something. **Build it.**

Write the utility that does not exist.  
Maintain the library everyone else abandoned.  
Port the software nobody bothered to port.  
Improve Wine compatibility.  
Write the Linux-native application that replaces another proprietary dependency.

Every new tool makes Linux more useful.  
Every compatibility improvement makes Windows less necessary.  
Every abandoned library brought back to life removes another piece of technological dependency.

**You don't defeat corporate software by refusing to use technology. You defeat it by making alternatives so good that people no longer need the corporation behind it.**

And to the AI haters and bigots:

**AI did not steal your work. Corporations did. Governments allowed.**  
**AI is not firing you. Corporate managers looking for higher profits are.**

The technology is not inherently the enemy. The question is who controls it, who benefits from it, and what we choose to build with it.

In the right hands, AI should replace dangerous work, eliminate pointless bureaucracy, and remove the miserable layers of unnecessary human busywork that make everyone's lives worse.

**Use the tools. Build the alternatives. Break the dependencies.**
